## Why YouTube Shorts for business keep bringing views

YouTube is a search engine as much as a social network. People type questions into it every day: how to bleed a radiator, why a sourdough loaf comes out flat, what to plant in a shady garden. Shorts appear in those results as well as in the Shorts feed, and Google shows short videos in some of its own results too. A Short that answers a real question can be found next month and next year by people who have never heard of you.

The audience is huge: in his 2026 letter, YouTube's chief executive wrote that Shorts average 200 billion daily views ([YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026/)). You only need a tiny share of that, as long as it is the right share.

Compare that with Instagram, TikTok or Facebook. There, you have to keep catching people's attention, post after post, or you disappear from their feed: those platforms turn you into an unpaid employee who begs to be seen. YouTube Shorts and {{< a key="pinterest-for-business" >}}Pinterest for business{{< /a >}} work the other way round. What you post there is evergreen and keeps working for you, which is why I see them as probably your best allies in 2026.

## Who YouTube Shorts suit, and who should skip them

Shorts reward businesses that can show or explain something in under a minute. Unlike Pinterest, which needs a picture worth saving, they also work for businesses that sell advice, because people go to YouTube for answers.

Shorts suit you well if you are in one of these groups:

- **Trades and home services:** a before-and-after, a common fault explained, a small job customers can do themselves.
- **Food, beauty, fitness, crafts and shops:** the product being made or used, a technique, a quick tip.
- **Advisers and professionals** such as accountants, estate agents, solicitors or driving instructors: one frequent question answered in 40 seconds.
- **Software and online services:** one feature shown, one problem solved.

Skip them, or keep them for later, in three cases:

- **You cannot keep up one video a week for three months.** A channel with six Shorts followed by silence does very little.
- **You sell to a short list of companies you could name today.** A short, plain-text cold email reaches them faster than any video; my {{< a key="b2b-cold-emailing-guide" >}}B2B cold emailing guide{{< /a >}} explains how.
- **Another channel already brings you clients.** It is easy to fall for the shiny new channel everyone talks about; a small business gets more from the channels it already runs well.

## The YouTube Shorts rules in 2026: length, views and links

Three changes shape how a business should use Shorts today:

| Rule | Since | What it changes for you |
|---|---|---|
| Shorts can last up to 3 minutes, in a square or vertical format | 15 October 2024 | Room for a full answer, though one idea per Short still works best |
| A view counts every time a Short starts or replays | 31 March 2025 | View counts look bigger than the real audience |
| Links in Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable | 31 August 2023 | You need another path to your website |

**Length.** YouTube announced the move to 3 minutes on its [official blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/). I would still keep most business Shorts under a minute: one question, one answer.

**Views.** A play or a replay now counts as a view, with no minimum watch time. The previous, stricter count remains in YouTube Analytics as **engaged views**, and the Partner Programme relies on a stricter measure than the public counter. A counter showing 3,000 views does not mean 3,000 people watched, so judge your Shorts on engaged views, new subscribers and enquiries.

**Links.** YouTube removed clickable links from Shorts descriptions and comments to fight spam and scams. Some guides published in 2026 say description links work again, others say they do not, and I found no announcement from YouTube, so plan as if a Short carries no clickable link. Links on your channel page and in the descriptions of long videos still work; a section below shows how to use them.

**What YouTube announced in 2026.** At [Made on YouTube 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/innovation-youtube-era-made-on-viewers-creators/), YouTube presented Shorts series, with seasons and episodes, and a Gemini-powered editing assistant that follows written instructions to trim speech, add music or text, and reorder clips. New features often reach accounts and countries gradually, so check your app before counting on them. A weekly series of tips is the obvious use for a business.

## What to film: YouTube Shorts ideas for a small business

The best business Shorts answer a question your customers already ask. Start a list today: every question from a phone call, an email or a quote request is a Short waiting to be filmed.

Six formats work for almost any trade:

1. **The question everyone asks.** "How long does a boiler service take?" Give the answer in your first sentence, then explain.
2. **The mistake to avoid.** One error you see every week, and how to avoid it.
3. **Before and after.** The result of a job in ten seconds, with one line on what made the difference.
4. **The process in three steps.** How you make, fix or prepare something.
5. **Myth or fact.** One belief from your trade, corrected in plain words.
6. **The product in use.** Not on a white background: in someone's hands, doing its job.

Two rules apply to all of them. Show the result or say the answer in the first two seconds, because viewers swipe. And if you work locally, say your town and put it in the title: Shorts reach people far beyond your area, and the name of the town tells the right viewers that you can help them.

## A realistic routine for filming Shorts alone with a phone

You do not need a studio. A phone, a window, a clip-on microphone and less than two hours a week are enough. Here is the routine I would suggest to an owner who does everything alone:

1. **Plan once a month (30 minutes).** Pick eight questions from your list. Type the start of each one into YouTube's search bar: the suggestions that appear show how people phrase it.
2. **Film every other week (1 hour).** Record four Shorts in one session, in the same place. Hold the phone vertically at eye level, face a window and clip the microphone on. Film each answer two or three times and keep the best take.
3. **Edit each Short (about 20 minutes).** Cut the pauses, put the key sentence on screen for people who watch without sound, and end with your business name. YouTube's own editor or Canva, which I use every day for small visual jobs, is enough; my guide on {{< a key="canva-pro-review" >}}whether Canva Pro is worth it{{< /a >}} explains what the free plan covers.
4. **Schedule (10 minutes a week).** Upload and schedule two Shorts a week, as shown below.
5. **Answer comments (10 minutes a week).** The questions people leave there are your next Shorts.

That makes eight Shorts a month, and steady beats intense: two a week for six months will do more than twenty in a fortnight.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}**Spend on sound before image.** A slightly soft picture is easier to forgive than a voice lost in a room's echo. If you buy one thing for Shorts, make it a clip-on microphone for your phone.{{< /callout >}}

## Titles, descriptions and the path to your website

Because people find Shorts through search, the title does the job a keyword does on a web page. Use the words your customers type, not your brochure's: "Why is my boiler losing pressure?" matches a real search, "Expert heating advice" does not. Finding the words people search before they buy is the core of my {{< a page="/services/seo" >}}SEO work{{< /a >}}, and the habit works on YouTube too.

Start the description with the same question in plain words, then add your business name and your town. Two or three relevant hashtags are enough.

Since you cannot count on a clickable link in a Short, build a path from the Short to your website:

1. **Your channel page.** Viewers who tap your channel name from a Short land there. In YouTube Studio, open **Customisation** and put your website first in your channel's links, with a clear title: the first link is the one visible at a glance on your channel page.
2. **Your name, said and shown.** End each Short with your domain name, out loud and on screen. A short domain that is easy to spell helps a lot here.
3. **A longer video.** When a Short raises more questions, answer them in a longer video: its description can carry clickable links. If your upload screen offers a related video option for the Short, use it to point to that video.

To see these visits in your website analytics, add tracking parameters to the link on your channel:

```text
https://www.yourbusiness.co.uk/?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=channel
```

## How to schedule YouTube Shorts for free in YouTube Studio

You do not need a paid tool to publish on a schedule: YouTube Studio does it for free from a computer.

1. Save the finished video on your computer: vertical or square, 3 minutes or less.
2. In YouTube Studio, click **Create**, then **Upload videos**, and select the file.
3. In **Details**, add the title and description, and answer the audience question: for a business video, the answer is usually **No, it's not made for kids**.
4. Continue to the **Visibility** step, choose **Schedule** and set the date and time.
5. Confirm with the **Schedule** button. The Short then waits on the **Content** page, under the **Shorts** tab, until its publication time.

A steady rhythm matters more than a perfect hour. If you also post on Pinterest, Instagram or TikTok, a scheduling tool can put everything in one calendar. I like Metricool, which also shows what successful competitors publish and how your accounts grow, with a generous number of accounts on its first tier; Postly is good enough to get going. ## Shorts, TikTok or Instagram Reels: film once, post in three places

The same vertical video can go on YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok. Upload your original file each time, rather than a copy with another app's watermark, and rewrite the title and caption for each platform.

Put most of your care into YouTube, though. In my view, people on TikTok are not really there to buy, which makes it mainly a brand awareness channel. Instagram and Facebook ads can bring sales, but without ads those networks, like TikTok, ask you to keep posting to stay visible. ## How to tell if your YouTube Shorts are working

Give it three months before you judge, then look at four things:

- **Engaged views and subscribers** gained per Short, in the **Analytics** section of YouTube Studio (if engaged views do not show, switch to advanced mode), rather than the public view count.
- **Where views come from.** Views from **YouTube search** show that a Short answers something people look for: those are the views that keep coming.
- **Visits from YouTube** in your website analytics, thanks to the tracked link on your channel.
- **What new customers tell you.** Ask every new customer how they found you, and note each mention of your videos.

Drop the formats that bring neither search views nor enquiries. Followers are not the goal; enquiries are.

## Your first month of YouTube Shorts: a checklist

1. **Week 1:** set up the channel under your business name, with a two-line description that names your town and a tracked link to your website.
2. **Week 2:** list 20 customer questions and check how people phrase them in YouTube's search bar.
3. **Week 3:** film, edit and schedule your first four Shorts, two a week.
4. **Week 4:** film the next four and add the questions from comments to your list.
5. **After three months:** film more on the topics that bring search views and enquiries, and turn the most popular question into a longer video.
