Calendly vs YouCanBookMe is a closer match than the size of the two companies suggests. Both connect to your calendar, give you a booking page that shows only your free slots, and let a client book a call or a visit without five emails to agree on a time. I use YouCanBookMe: it does everything I need from Calendly, I find my way around it easily, and its cheapest paid plan costs a bit less.

Calendly is good too, don't get me wrong. It is the bigger name, it connects natively to more tools, and for a sales team that works inside a CRM all day, it is the one I would set up first. This comparison shows where each one wins, with prices at the time of writing (October 2026).

## What both tools do equally well

For the everyday job, I would not steer anyone towards one or the other on features alone. Both of them:

- read your Google or Microsoft calendar and offer only the slots where you are free, so nobody books over the dentist appointment you added last week;
- apply your rules: working hours, a buffer before and after each appointment, and a minimum notice so that no one books you for ten minutes from now;
- handle time zones: a client in London sees your 10:00 Paris slot as 9:00, and the invitation lands at the right hour in both calendars;
- ask your own questions in the booking form: name, email, phone number, the address of a site visit, the reason for the call;
- send a confirmation email and a calendar invitation, with links to cancel or reschedule without writing to you.

If that list covers what you need, **the choice comes down to price, how each tool feels to use, and a few team features**. Solo owners can mostly ignore the last part.

### Which one is cheaper

For one person who has outgrown the free plan and connects one or two calendars, YouCanBookMe: Individual costs $9 a month against $12 for Calendly Standard, 25 per cent less. For a team paying month by month, YouCanBookMe again, by $2 per person. On yearly billing, the two team plans cost almost the same ($16 and $16.20).

Like for like, Calendly is slightly cheaper. With six connected calendars, Standard costs $12 a month against $13 for YouCanBookMe Professional, or $10 against $11.70 on yearly billing. So "a bit cheaper" holds for a solo owner with one or two calendars and for a team paying monthly, not for every plan. **Compare the plan you need, not the cheapest line of each table.**

## Where they differ: payments, reminders and booking forms

### Payments

Both can charge the client at the moment of booking, which suits paid consultations and discourages no-shows. Calendly takes payment through Stripe or PayPal from the Standard plan, and YouCanBookMe through Stripe. If your clients expect to pay with PayPal, Calendly has it built in.

### Reminders

Calendly sends automated reminders and follow-up emails through its workflows, from the Standard plan. YouCanBookMe sends reminder emails as well, and its text-message reminders run on SMS credits that you buy on top of the subscription. If your clients live on their phones, **budget for those credits** before you compare monthly prices.

### Booking forms

Both let you write your own questions, so you arrive at the call already knowing the address of the site visit or the size of the project. Calendly's Teams plan goes further with routing forms: the answers decide where the person lands, for example a sales call for a new project and a support call for an existing client.

## Team features and integrations: where Calendly leads

Both tools share appointments across a team on their Teams plans. Round robin sends each new booking to the next available person, so the workload stays even and no prospect waits for one busy colleague.

Beyond that, Calendly has the edge for a sales team:

- routing forms, which qualify a lead before it reaches anyone's calendar;
- CRM integrations built in, with Salesforce on the Teams plan;
- a much longer list of ready-made connections, so fewer gaps to fill with Zapier or custom code.

YouCanBookMe covers the essentials for a small team: round robin on its Teams plan, and a Zapier connection to carry booking details to your other tools. If your team books calls and Zapier can take them where they need to go, that is enough. **If your sales process runs inside Salesforce, choose Calendly.** I would rather tell you that now than after you have spent an afternoon setting up the other one.

## Why I use YouCanBookMe

My reasons are short, and none of them is a criticism of Calendly.

1. It does what I need from Calendly. Booking pages, calendar checks, buffers, reminders, a booking form and an embed on a website: for the way I work, nothing is missing.
2. It is easy to find your way around. I rarely have to hunt for a setting.
3. The team behind it is smaller, which I count in its favour.
4. It is a bit cheaper for a solo owner with one or two calendars, as the tables above show.

None of this makes Calendly a bad choice. If you already use it and it works, keep it. Switching tools to save a few dollars a month is the kind of shiny-object chase I warn small business owners about: **the time is better spent on your website or your prospects**.

## Calendly or YouCanBookMe: which one to choose

Choose Calendly if:

- your team sells, and leads need routing to the right person;
- your CRM is Salesforce and you want the connection built in;
- you need many event types on a single seat, since Standard sets no limit.

Choose YouCanBookMe if:

- you work alone or in a small team, with one to a few kinds of appointment;
- you want the cheapest paid plan that does the job, or 20 per cent off for paying two years upfront;
- you want settings that are easy to find, which is how YouCanBookMe feels to me.

## Before you sign up: a short checklist

1. List what people book with you: discovery calls, site visits, consultations. That number tells you how many booking pages or event types you need.
2. Count the calendars to check for conflicts: work, personal, a shared team calendar. One is free on both tools; more means a paid plan.
3. Decide whether clients pay when they book: both take card payments through Stripe, and Calendly also offers PayPal.
4. Decide where the link goes. Keep it out of a first cold email, though: I never put links in cold emails, only a phone number, because plain text without links gets delivered more reliably. Save the booking link for the replies; my {{< a key="b2b-cold-emailing-guide" >}}B2B cold emailing guide{{< /a >}} covers the rest of the method.
5. Plan the embed. A link on a button works on every site.
