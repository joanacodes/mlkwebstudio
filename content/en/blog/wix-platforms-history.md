Wix Studio vs Wix Editor is the first decision to make before you build on Wix, and since January 2026 there has been a third option, Wix Harmony. Having three editors from one company is confusing, and it is nothing new: since its first editor in 2007, Wix has kept launching, renaming and retiring platforms.

I know Wix well. I have tried its platforms one after another and even gone to Wix events, so this is an opinion piece as much as a comparison: what each editor is for, how Wix ended up with three of them, and why I do not recommend any of them to an owner who will edit the site alone.

If you are still comparing platforms from scratch, start with my {{< a key="small-business-website-guide" >}}guide to small business websites{{< /a >}}.

## Wix Studio vs Wix Editor vs Harmony at a glance

All three run on Wix's hosting, but they are separate products, and a site generally stays in the editor it was built in.

| | Wix Editor | Wix Studio | Wix Harmony |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | The classic drag-and-drop builder | The professional platform that replaced Editor X | Wix's newest editor, built around an AI agent |
| Built for | Owners who build their own site | Agencies and freelancers, in Wix's own words | Anyone who wants AI to draft the site, then edit by hand |
| How you design | Drag elements anywhere on a desktop page, then adjust a separate mobile view | Responsive layouts with breakpoints for desktop, tablet and mobile | Describe changes to Aria, the AI agent, and fine-tune with drag and drop |
| Custom code | Yes, with Velo | Yes, with Velo and more developer tools | No Velo at the time of writing |
| Moving a Wix Editor site to it | Not applicable | No automatic move: plan a rebuild of the design | Not possible, according to Wix |

The last row is the one to remember. Wix says an existing Wix Editor site cannot be migrated to Harmony ([Wix's comparison of the two editors](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-harmony-editor-the-difference-between-wix-editor-and-wix-harmony)), and when it retired Editor X, it moved those sites to Wix Studio but left classic Wix Editor sites where they were. **Choosing an editor means choosing where your site will live**, so it pays to get it right the first time.

## Who each Wix editor is for

### The Wix Editor, for building your own site

The Wix Editor is what most people picture when they think of Wix: you pick a template, then drag text, images and buttons anywhere on the page. That freedom is what attracts beginners. It is also the catch: you design for desktop, the mobile version needs its own adjustments, and a page laid out element by element takes patience to keep tidy.

It suits a personal project or a first try. For a business site you will update for years, read my verdict below first.

### Wix Studio, for agencies, freelancers and specialists

Wix announced Wix Studio in August 2023 as a platform for agencies and freelancers. You build responsive layouts with breakpoints, so one page adapts to desktop, tablet and mobile, and you get more developer tools and a workspace designed to manage several client sites.

That makes it the right Wix for a specialist, and a steep climb for everyone else.

### Wix Harmony, for building with AI

Harmony launched on 21 January 2026. You tell Aria, Wix's AI agent, what you want; it builds and edits the site, and you can still adjust the result by hand with drag and drop. It is a third editor next to the other two, not an upgrade of either.

It is also the youngest, and features are still being added. Check Wix's comparison page for what it can do today, and test it on a throwaway site before you build your business on it.

## From Flash to Harmony: how Wix's editors evolved

Wix was founded in Tel Aviv in 2006. Since then, its history has been a series of editors, each presented as the new way to build a website:

| When | What changed |
|---|---|
| 2007 | The first Wix editor, built in Flash, opens in beta |
| March 2012 | An HTML5 editor replaces the Flash one; today's Wix Editor descends from it |
| June 2016 | Wix ADI (Artificial Design Intelligence) builds a site from your answers to a few questions |
| December 2017 | Wix Code adds custom code and databases; it becomes Corvid in 2019, then Velo in January 2021 |
| July 2020 | Editor X, an advanced responsive editor for designers and agencies, opens to the public; it leaves beta in February 2021 |
| August 2023 | Wix announces Wix Studio, and opens it to everyone in October |
| March 2024 | The AI Website Builder replaces ADI, which Wix stops supporting in November 2024 |
| By January 2025 | Every Editor X site has been moved to the Studio Editor ([Wix's migration FAQ](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-studio-faqs-migrating-from-editor-x-to-the-studio-editor)) |
| June 2025 | Wix buys Base44, a vibe-coding platform that builds apps from plain-language prompts |
| January 2026 | Wix Harmony launches, with Aria |

Read the table as a user rather than an investor and the pattern is plain: three of these platforms, the Flash editor, ADI and Editor X, no longer exist, and the coding tool changed its name twice before Wix began steering developers towards its JavaScript SDK. Each change was decided by Wix, on Wix's timetable.

## My honest verdict on Wix

Let me start with what Wix does well. On paper, it is the absolute best: templates for every trade, an editor that lets you put anything anywhere, bookings, online shops, a large app market, AI tools throughout, and hosting in the same subscription. I have also seen web designers make great things with it, and those designers specialise in Wix.

In practice, I find it the worst. I have used it on different devices, in different homes, on different Wi-Fi connections and in different countries, and the struggle has always been the same: **the editor lags**. To my eyes, Wix has got worse, not better.

The other problem is who ends up using it. Wix is very good at attracting people who have never been shown the alternatives, and it is mostly solo business owners who fall into that trap.

So my advice to founders is short:

- If you want to edit the site yourself, do not use Wix.
- If you still want Wix, go with Wix Studio, hire a very good specialist, one whose work will not make your company look unserious, and never touch the site again. Let them make every change, however small.

Your website is often a prospect's first impression of you, and my guide to making your {{< a key="professional-looking-website" >}}website look professional{{< /a >}} covers what visitors judge in the first seconds.

One distinction matters. The lag I describe is in the editor, where you work. For the site your visitors see, measure rather than trust anyone's impression, mine included: my guide to {{< a key="core-web-vitals-speed" >}}Core Web Vitals{{< /a >}} shows how to test a page in a few minutes.

## The hidden cost of every new Wix editor

The lag is not the only cost. What I found most confusing was the churn: you learn how a new platform works, see it merged into another one or removed, then start learning again.

For a small business, that churn costs three things:

1. Your time. A new editor means new menus, new layout logic and new habits, and the hours spent learning them come out of your working week.
2. Your control. When Wix retires a platform, your site moves on Wix's timetable, not yours.
3. Your way out. You cannot take a Wix site to another host, so the only alternative to keeping up is a rebuild.

None of this appears in the monthly price. On a website builder, keeping up with the platform is part of {{< a key="website-maintenance" >}}website maintenance{{< /a >}}, alongside the content, the security and the SEO.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}Whoever builds your Wix site, keep the site in a Wix account in your company's name, register the domain in that name too, and invite the specialist as a collaborator. If they build it in their own account, agree in writing when they will transfer it to you: Wix has a **Transfer site** option, and the transfer only goes through once you accept it from your own account.{{< /callout >}}

## Should you use Wix? A checklist before you choose

Base the decision on who will edit the site, not on the feature list:

- You will edit the site yourself: choose a builder you can use without fear. For most owners, I recommend Squarespace, the easiest builder I know, and my {{< a key="squarespace-vs-wordpress" >}}Squarespace vs WordPress comparison{{< /a >}} explains why.
- Someone will make every change for you: Wix Studio is a sound choice if that person specialises in it. Ask which editor they build in, and why.
- You want a simple brochure site and are at ease with AI tools: it is now easier to {{< a key="build-website-with-ai" >}}build a website with AI{{< /a >}} and host it on GitHub Pages or Spaceship than to learn a Wix editor. GitHub Pages is not meant for an online shop.
- You are on the Wix Editor and eyeing Studio or Harmony: neither will take your site as it is, so budget for a rebuild, not a switch.
- Your Wix site already works and brings in enquiries: keep it. Test its speed, keep the SEO basics in order, and rebuild only when the site holds the business back.
