The Instagram algorithm in 2026 favours three things: original content, posts that people watch to the end, and posts they send to someone else. Two changes also reshape how people find you: anyone using Instagram in English can now see and edit the topics it shows them, and captions are capped at five hashtags. If your reach has dropped this year, one of the dated changes below may explain it.

## Instagram algorithm 2026: how it ranks your posts

Instagram does not run one algorithm. The home feed, Stories, Reels and Explore each rank posts in their own way, as [Instagram has explained for years](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), and each one shows your post to a different audience:

| Part of the app | Who sees your post | What helps most |
|---|---|---|
| Home feed | Mostly your followers, mixed with recommended posts | Likes, comments and shares from people who know you |
| Stories | Your followers | Followers who often view or reply to your Stories |
| Reels | Mostly people who do not follow you | Watch time and shares |
| Explore | People who do not follow you | Posts close to what they already like, save and share |

Adam Mosseri, who runs Instagram, has named the three signals that weigh most: watch time, sends per reach (the share of viewers who send your post to someone in a private message) and likes per reach. Sends count most when Instagram decides whether to show a post to people who do not follow you. A post that a customer forwards to a friend tells Instagram far more than a polite like.

Since December 2025, users also steer the algorithm themselves. [Your Algorithm](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/reels-algorithm-control) lists the topics Instagram thinks each person cares about and lets them ask for more or less of each, or type in a new one. Instagram describes it as one system, so a change updates recommendations across the app. For a business, that makes **being clearly about one thing** more important than before.

### July 2025: your public posts can appear in Google

Since 10 July 2025, search engines such as Google can show public photos and videos from professional accounts held by adults. A switch in your account privacy settings turns this off. For most businesses I would leave it on and write each caption the way you would write a line on your website: what the post shows, where, and for whom, in your customers' own words.

### December 2025: Your Algorithm and the five-hashtag cap

Instagram tested Your Algorithm on Reels in October 2025, launched it in the US on 10 December 2025, then opened it to all English-speaking users. It opens from an icon at the top right of Reels. In 2026 it reached Explore in the spring and then the home feed, and in June Instagram was testing more ways to adjust it.

The same month, Instagram began limiting posts and Reels to five hashtags each, rolled out gradually after a test that held some users to three. Its reasoning: a few targeted hashtags work better than a long list of generic ones (the option to follow a hashtag had already gone). Pick three to five that describe the post the way a customer would search for it: your trade, your town, the subject. A florist in Leeds is better served by #leedsflorist and #weddingflowers than by #love or #instagood.

### April 2026: a stricter originality rule

Instagram has pushed reposted and watermarked videos down for years. In April 2026 it changed how it judges whole accounts: it looks at the last 30 days on a rolling basis, and an account whose posts are mostly reposts of other people's content is treated as an aggregator and stops being recommended to people who do not follow it. Once most of its recent posts are original again, it becomes eligible again.

Sharing someone else's material is still fine when you add something of your own, and Instagram points to its built-in tools for this: Collab posts, Remix and the paid partnership label. When you work with a supplier or a neighbouring business, publish a Collab post together rather than reposting theirs: it appears on both profiles.

### September 2026: Adam Mosseri's advice on reach

In a series of videos in September 2026, Mosseri said Instagram gives more weight to original content from the person who made it than to accounts that repost it. He told creators to keep experimenting, because trends change, and suggested Trial Reels. He has also said that sharing your own post to your Stories does not meaningfully change its reach, and that most reach "hacks" do not work.

Trial Reels, available to professional accounts, are shown first to people who do not follow you; you then decide whether to share them with your followers. They are a safe way to test a new format before your regular audience sees it.

## What to change in your Instagram routine

Put together, the 2026 changes reward a small business that does a few simple things consistently:

1. **Pick one subject and stay on it.** People now choose their topics, and an account that posts about everything fits none of them. I have seen the same rule with websites: a client's site that offered too many building trades did not work at all, while the one that sells very specific services ranks first or second on Google.
2. **Make posts people want to send.** A tip a customer will forward, a before-and-after, a common question answered, a local recommendation: the kind of post someone sends to a friend with "we need this".
3. **Show your own work.** Use your photos and videos, edited in Instagram or Edits, rather than reposts or videos carrying another app's watermark.
4. **Write captions for search.** Say what, where and for whom in the first line, and tag your location: it costs nothing, and public posts tagged at a place can appear on Instagram Map, which opened in the US in August 2025 and is reaching other countries in stages.
5. **Plan in batches.** Prepare a week or two at a time and schedule it. Professional accounts can schedule posts for free in Instagram and in Meta Business Suite, and a tool such as Metricool or Postly puts several networks in one calendar. Metricool also shows what successful competitors publish, which beats guessing; my comparison of {{< a key="social-media-scheduling-tools" >}}social media scheduling tools{{< /a >}} covers both.
6. **Give every post a path to your website.** Followers live on Instagram's terms; enquiries arrive on your website. Put your site first in your bio links, with tracking so the visits show up in your analytics:

```text
https://www.yourbusiness.co.uk/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio
```

The page people land on should answer their question and make it easy to call or write to you. A site that turns those visits into enquiries is what I focus on in my {{< a page="/services/websites" >}}website projects{{< /a >}}.

## Is Instagram worth it for a small business in 2026?

My honest answer: yes, if you have time, an advertising budget, or both. Instagram, like TikTok and Facebook, asks you to keep catching people's attention, post after post, for a long time; stop, and you fade from their feeds. These platforms turn owners into unpaid staff who have to beg to be seen. Even a {{< a key="google-business-profile-posts" >}}Google Business Profile{{< /a >}} is kinder to a busy owner.

If you have the budget, hire an agency with a strong, proven track record, and be patient: do not expect results straight away. Instagram and Facebook ads can bring both awareness and sales, but you need money to burn on A/B tests before you know which version works. The two mistakes I warn owners about are skipping research into the words your customers use, and skipping those tests; my guide to {{< a key="facebook-ads-mistakes" >}}Facebook ads mistakes{{< /a >}} covers both.

If you work alone, post less on Instagram and put the hours you save where a post keeps working: {{< a key="pinterest-for-business" >}}Pinterest{{< /a >}} and {{< a key="youtube-shorts-for-business" >}}YouTube Shorts{{< /a >}}, where something you publish this month can still bring visitors next year. Keep Instagram as a shop window: a few good posts that show your work and point to your website.

And do not chase followers for credibility. "I have 10k followers on Instagram" impresses less than "this magazine published an article about my business", and the second needs no followers at all: see {{< a key="get-press-coverage" >}}how to get press coverage{{< /a >}}.

## Your Instagram checklist for the next 30 days

1. **Count your reposts** over the last 30 days. If they make up most of your posts, replace them with your own content.
2. **Trim your hashtags** to three to five precise ones, including in the caption templates you copy and paste.
3. **Rewrite your bio** and the first line of your next captions: what you do, where, and for whom.
4. **Check the search engine setting** in your account privacy settings and decide whether Google may show your public posts.
5. **Try one Trial Reel** in a format you have never used, and look at its results before you share it with your followers.
6. **Put your website first** in your bio links, with the tracked link above.
7. **Set your weekly budget** in hours and in money. If the honest answer is "very little", publish fewer, better posts here and give the rest of your time to channels that keep working.
