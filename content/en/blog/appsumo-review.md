AppSumo is a marketplace that sells software deals, most of them lifetime deals: you pay once for a plan instead of paying every month. Of everything I have bought there, only one tool is something I use every single day: Reoon, an email verifier. So my answer to "is AppSumo worth it?" comes down to one test: did you need the tool before you saw the deal?

When the answer is yes, AppSumo can be very good value. When it is no, it works a bit like a casino: everything looks cheap, so purchases add up, and some of the tools you paid for "for life" may stop working one day.

This review explains how the deals, licence tiers and refunds work, what I use and what I missed, and the rules for buying without regret. It complements my guide to {{< a key="small-business-tools-guide" >}}small business marketing tools{{< /a >}}, the stack I use every day and what it costs.

## How AppSumo works: lifetime deals, licence tiers and codes

A lifetime deal is a one-off payment for a software plan that you keep for as long as the product exists. Software companies, often young ones, list a plan on AppSumo at a one-off price, usually for a limited time, to find users and raise cash quickly.

Most deals come in licence tiers. The first tier is the cheapest, and each tier above it adds users, credits or features. Older deals used codes instead: you bought two or three and stacked them to get a bigger plan. Either way, once you have paid, you activate the licence on the software company's own website, and from then on you use the tool like its other customers, within the limits of your tier. Many deals also ask you to do this within 60 days of purchase.

Each deal page sets out its terms: what the plan includes, the limits of each tier, which future updates come with it and the refund window. **Read them before you pay**: they are the contract, not the sales text above them.

Here is how a lifetime deal compares with a normal subscription.

| | Monthly subscription | AppSumo lifetime deal |
|---|---|---|
| How you pay | Every month or every year | Once |
| Cost over time | Keeps adding up | Fixed at the deal price |
| New features | Come with the plan you pay for | Only those the deal terms include |
| If you stop using the tool | You cancel and stop paying | The money is already spent |
| If the company closes | You stop paying | The deal ends with it |

{{< callout type="warning" >}}"Lifetime" means the life of the product, not yours. If the company closes, **the deal ends with it**.{{< /callout >}}

## AppSumo refunds: the 60-day window and its limits

At the time of writing (October 2026), most deals on [AppSumo](https://appsumo.com/) come with a 60-day refund window, counted from the date of purchase. The window is written in each deal's terms, and a few listings follow other rules, so trust the deal page over any review, this one included.

Inside the window, you can ask AppSumo for your money back if the tool does not do the job. [AppSumo's help centre](https://help.appsumo.com/article/34-what-is-a-lifetime-deal) is clear that this window is the only time your purchase is fully protected by its money-back guarantee. If the company stops updating the tool or closes a year later, you may never see that money again, so **all your testing has to happen inside the window**.

Sixty days is shorter than it sounds. A tool bought on a Friday evening, to be "set up properly next month", can run out of refund time before you have opened it twice.

## Why AppSumo is a bit like a casino

AppSumo is a bit like a casino: you end up spending a lot because everything seems cheap, and not everything is worth it. Nobody forces you to buy; the pull comes from the way deals are presented.

- A one-off price does not feel like a commitment, so you skip the question you would ask of a subscription: will I use this every week?
- A deadline turns "maybe later" into "now", because once a deal ends, its price is gone.
- The saving is always on display: deal pages set the one-off price against the tool's normal price, often a yearly one, so every purchase feels like money saved.
- The next tier up always looks like better value per user or per credit.
- New deals keep arriving, so there is always another one to consider.

Each purchase is small. Together, **they add up to real money** spent on tools you rarely open, plus the hours spent setting them up and learning them. It is the same trap as {{< a key="shiny-object-syndrome" >}}shiny object syndrome{{< /a >}}: a new tool feels like progress, while the channels you already run are the ones that need your attention.

## The real risks of a lifetime deal

Some tools may stop working eventually. That is the risk the refund window does not cover, and it takes several forms:

1. The company closes. The deal ends with it, and you have to move your work and your data elsewhere.
2. Development slows down. Lifetime buyers pay once, so servers, support and new features have to be funded by new customers. When sales slow, updates can slow too.
3. New features go elsewhere. Depending on the deal terms, features added later can be kept for subscribers.
4. Your tier caps you. When you outgrow it, you pay the company's normal prices, if it still sells extra capacity.
5. Your data gets stuck. If a tool holds your clients' details or your content, check that you can export them before you rely on it.

None of this makes lifetime deals bad. It means **a deal is safer for a tool you could replace in an afternoon** than for the one your whole business runs on. An email verifier is a good example: if one closes, another can check your next list the same day.

## Is AppSumo worth it for you? Rules for buying without regret

AppSumo suits owners and small teams who know exactly which job they need a tool for, and want to stop paying every month for software they use every week. It does not suit browsing for ideas, or putting the core of your business on a tool from a company you know nothing about.

Before you buy, check the deal against these rules:

1. Write the need down before you open the site. **If the tool was not on your list before you saw the deal, close the tab.**
2. Choose tools for a job you do every week. Reoon passes this test for me: I use it every day.
3. Check the company: how long it has existed, whether it sells normal subscriptions outside AppSumo, when it last shipped an update, and how it answers the questions buyers ask on the deal page.
4. Buy the lowest tier that covers what you do today, not what you might need one day.
5. On the day you buy, activate the licence, put the refund deadline in your calendar and plan to test the tool on real work in the first two weeks.
6. Check the export: your data should be able to leave the tool in a standard format, such as CSV.
7. Let deals go. If you need the tool later, you can pay its normal price, and you will have kept the money from all the deals you did not need.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}Keep a simple list of what you bought, the date, the refund deadline and the last time you used each tool. **Read it before you buy anything new.**{{< /callout >}}

You do not need anyone's help to buy well on AppSumo: a list and a calendar reminder are enough.
