The honest answer to "how much does a website cost in the UK?" depends on what the site has to do, who writes it and who looks after it once it is live. Those three choices decide whether you pay a subscription and your own evenings, or a project fee to a freelancer, a studio or an agency, and how much the site costs you every year after launch.

I will not give you a made-up UK average. I found no independent survey of UK website prices, so this guide sticks to what I can check: published platform prices, a large survey from France, where I also work, the yearly bills people forget, and how I price my own projects.

If you are planning the whole project, from choosing a platform to launch day, start with my {{< a key="small-business-website-guide" >}}guide to small business websites{{< /a >}}.

## How much does a website cost in the UK, route by route

There are five ways to get a business website. Two cost mostly your time, plus a subscription or a domain name. The other three are a project fee, then running costs.

| Route | What you pay | Suits | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website builder, built by you | A subscription: £12 to £79 a month on Squarespace, billed annually | Owners who will edit the site themselves | You write, design and maintain it |
| AI-built site on free hosting | Your domain name, plus your time | Hands-on owners who need a simple brochure site | Not for a shop, and the code is yours to look after |
| Freelancer | A one-off project fee, then running costs | A clear brief and a well-defined project | Everything rests on one person |
| Small studio | A fixed price per project, then running costs | Design, copy and SEO groundwork in one place | Ask what happens after launch |
| Agency | A project fee, then running costs | Larger sites with several people to coordinate | You also pay for the team and its process |

At the time of writing (October 2026), UK pricing guides list Squarespace at £12 (Basic), £17 (Core), £29 (Plus) and £79 (Advanced) a month on annual billing, VAT included. Those figures may predate the rise Squarespace announced in July 2026: in the US, Basic went from $16 to $19, Core from $23 to $29 and Plus from $39 to $49 a month on annual billing, while Advanced stayed at $99. I found no confirmed new UK prices, so check the [official pricing page](https://www.squarespace.com/pricing) before you subscribe. Code injection, which I use to add custom code for clients, starts with Core.

For the build itself, the most solid figures I know come from France. [La Fabrique du Net's July 2026 price survey](https://www.lafabriquedunet.fr/agences/pages/agences-site-internet/tarifs) gathers 1,312 project budgets declared by 175 agencies: **half of the brochure sites cost between €2,000 and €8,000** (roughly £1,700 to £7,000), with a median below €4,000, and the median for all types of site is €5,200. These are agency prices in another market, so treat them as an order of magnitude, not as a UK price list.

## What drives the price of a website

Two quotes for "a five-page website" can be far apart because they do not cover the same work. These are the lines that move the price.

### Pages and page templates

A home page, four service pages and a contact page make one project. Add a blog, a portfolio, a page for each town you serve or a second language, and the design, the writing and the SEO work grow with them. Pages built from one template, such as product sheets, cost less each than pages designed one by one.

### Who writes the words

Copywriting is often the line a cheap quote leaves to you. If the price assumes you will supply every text, count the evenings it would take you to write ten pages that persuade people to call and that rank on Google. In my own projects, SEO research and copywriting are a full stage of the work, not an afterthought.

### Photos and visual identity

For a website, I need pictures from the client; otherwise I generate or buy them. Your own photos of your team, your premises and your work beat any stock image, and they cost nothing extra if you already have them. A logo and a visual identity are a separate piece of work, so check whether the quote includes them.

### Features beyond pages

Contact forms are standard. Online booking, payments, a shop, a members' area, a price calculator or a link to your customer software each add design, building and testing time. Some come as a builder feature or an extension; others need custom code, or a small web app built into the site.

### The platform, and who maintains it

Squarespace includes hosting, security and updates in the subscription. WordPress software is free, but hosting and premium plugins are not, and someone has to run the updates. A custom-coded site usually needs a developer for anything beyond simple content changes. My {{< a key="squarespace-vs-wordpress" >}}Squarespace vs WordPress comparison{{< /a >}} goes through these trade-offs in detail.

## The running costs that come back every year

The build is paid once. These lines come back every year, whoever builds the site:

| Cost | What to expect (October 2026) | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | A .co.uk costs about £4 a year ([$5.42 at Spaceship](https://www.spaceship.com/domains/)), at registration and at renewal | The renewal price, not the first-year offer |
| Subscription or hosting | £144 to £948 a year for Squarespace on annual billing | WordPress hosting is paid separately and renews too |
| Business email | Google Workspace Business Starter: £5.90 per user per month on an annual plan ($7 in the US), or £7 on the flexible plan ($8.40), before VAT | It is charged per mailbox |
| Maintenance | Your time, or a specialist's monthly fee | It weighs most on WordPress |
| SEO | Your time, or ongoing work you pay for | It is separate from the build |

Google lists its prices by country on its [help page on payment plans](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/billing/compare-flexible-and-annual-fixed-term-payment-plans). At those prices, a Squarespace site you build yourself on the Core plan (£204 a year), with a .co.uk domain and one Google Workspace mailbox (£70.80 a year), comes to about £280 a year before VAT on the mailbox, and before your own time.

Watch renewal prices in particular. Some providers advertise a cheap first year and charge much more afterwards; in my experience, Spaceship and Namecheap do not. Email is the other line people forget: I used to run email on Microsoft and found it a pain, which has not been the case with Namecheap and Spaceship. My guide to {{< a key="domain-hosting-email-guide" >}}domains, hosting and email{{< /a >}} explains how the three fit together.

## What a cheap website really costs

A low quote is not always the cheap option. These are the places where I see the bill come back.

### WordPress with nobody looking after it

Every client who has called me in an emergency called about a WordPress site. Pilotech, an air conditioning and heating installer in the Paris region, called me when its WordPress site was hacked: it needed the old site recovered and a new one built. The software costs nothing; you pay for the updates, or for the repairs when nobody runs them.

### A site that looks cheap

I want my clients' prospects to take them seriously from the first page, even to be a little intimidated. A site that looks amateur invites haggling: if a prospect doubts that you are a serious company, they also doubt that you are worth your price. My guide on how to {{< a key="professional-looking-website" >}}make your website look professional{{< /a >}} shows what changes that first impression.

### A free site from a friend or a relative

A website is not a one-time gift. Whoever builds it for free signs up for maintenance for life, unless you agree up front that changes will cost something, and free sites attract endless edit requests. My guide to {{< a key="website-maintenance" >}}website maintenance{{< /a >}} covers what upkeep involves and what it costs.

### A powerful tool you have to learn alone

Wix Studio and Webflow can produce excellent sites in a specialist's hands. For a solo owner who has to learn them alone, they cost hours, and the Wix editor has always felt laggy to me, on every device and connection I have tried. If you choose either, budget for a specialist as well.

## How to compare website quotes

Send the same brief to every provider, then compare the answers line by line:

1. Pages: how many are designed, and how many are written and filled in for you?
2. Copy: who writes the texts, and is keyword research included?
3. Photos and visual identity: included, bought, generated, or yours to supply?
4. SEO: what is done before launch, and what is left for later?
5. Platform: which one, and will you be able to edit the content yourself?
6. Revisions: how many rounds, and what counts as an extra?
7. After launch: what support, for how long, and at what price?
8. Year two: subscription or hosting, domain, email and maintenance, in writing.
9. Payment: deposit, stages and payment options.

{{< callout type="tip" >}}Ask every provider for the cost of **year two** in writing. A quote that only covers the build shows you part of the budget, not all of it.{{< /callout >}}

## Your website budget checklist

Before you ask anyone for a quote:

1. Write down what the site must bring in: calls, quote requests, bookings or sales.
2. List the pages: one per service, plus home, about, contact and legal pages.
3. Decide who will edit the site after launch, because that decides the platform.
4. Gather your photos, your logo and any texts you already have.
5. Set two budgets: one for the build, and one for every year after it.
6. Send the same brief to two or three providers and compare their answers with the list above.

If you are hands-on and only need a simple brochure site, you can {{< a key="build-website-with-ai" >}}build a website with AI{{< /a >}} and host it for free on GitHub Pages, within its limits: your main costs are then the domain and a few evenings, and GitHub Pages is not meant for a shop. If you will edit the site yourself, a builder subscription is a cost you know in advance. And if the site has to bring in clients from Google from the start, put the money into the copy and the SEO groundwork, because that is what turns a website into enquiries.
